How it works and how far to trust it

for teachers

How it decides

No model is involved. Each sentence (or poem line) is scored on stock phrases, polished abstract vocabulary, connector openers, list and heading templates, and how much personal voice and concrete detail it carries.

The whole text is then judged on rhythm, specifics and grade-level fit. Style on its own is capped, so a flat but plain paragraph reads as inconclusive and not as AI.

Before you act on a result

  • Compare with the student’s earlier, in-class writing.
  • Check the document’s version history for a steady build-up, not one large paste.
  • Ask the student to explain a paragraph or define a word they used.
  • Set the grade level and, where it applies, English learner or Lab or technical.
  • Use a result to start a conversation, never as proof.

How far to trust it

    Known weak spots. AI told to sound casual, deeply rewritten AI, AI text edited by hand, poems, and very short answers. Formal human writing, such as methods sections, news reports and English-learner essays, can look machine-like. Under about 80 words it is a guess.

    Help

    Quick start
    1. Paste the writing into Text to check (15 words or more; 80+ works better, 150+ best).
    2. Open Settings and pick the grade level. Change Who wrote it for English learners or lab work.
    3. Press Check text. Read the verdict, then the sentences that are highlighted.
    4. Use Copy report to keep a summary.
    What each setting does
    • Type. Essay or poem. Auto-detect looks for short lines in stanzas. Poems are scored line by line on cliché imagery, abstract nouns, rhyme and line evenness.
    • Grade level. A younger writer using polished words is a stronger signal. Elementary and middle school also expect shorter sentences.
    • Who wrote it. English learner and Lab or technical turn down rhythm, formality and impersonal-voice signals, the ones that wrongly flag these writers.
    • How cautious. Cautious asks for more evidence before saying AI. Use it for anything graded.
    Reading the result
    • The percentage is a likelihood from style evidence, with a likely range beside it. Short texts have wide ranges.
    • The bar shows how much of the text, sentence by sentence, reads as AI, uncertain or human.
    • Highlights. Warm = reads as AI. Cool = reads as human. Underlined words raised the score. Tap any sentence to see why.
    • Why this result lists evidence on both sides. Before you rely on this lists reasons the result could be a false alarm.
    Humanizers and edited AI

    Tools that “humanize” AI text usually add casual openers (“Honestly,”), fragments, typos or swapped synonyms. The checker looks for casual touches sitting on top of stock AI wording and for odd thesaurus phrases, and then trusts the human-looking parts less. Deep rewrites can still get through.

    When a result can be wrong
    • Formal human writing (news, lab methods, legal or academic prose) can look machine-like.
    • English learners and neurodivergent writers are flagged more often by every detector.
    • Poems and very short texts carry little evidence.
    • Careful AI text with no stock phrases can score low.
    Good practice for teachers
    • Never use the score alone. Compare with in-class writing and version history.
    • Ask the student to talk through the piece.
    • Treat 'Inconclusive' as no information.
